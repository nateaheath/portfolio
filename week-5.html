ARHA 295 / Week 5

Color studies

Monday / Artist of the day

Ebony G. Patterson

Ebony G. Patterson is a Jamaican artist whose layered works combine photography, textiles, beads, glitter, and floral imagery. Her intricately embellished compositions explore visibility, mourning, and the relationship between beauty and violence. I am drawn to how her vivid colors and dense patterns attract the eye while partially concealing the figures within them, making looking closely an important part of encountering her work.

Artist and artwork information found at moniquemeloche.

On color

Patterson’s vivid pinks, reds, and greens initially make her compositions feel lush and celebratory. Repeated flowers and elaborate patterns draw my attention across the surface, but their density also makes the figures harder to distinguish. In the garden work shown above, bright red flowers stand out against dark foliage before the reclining figure becomes apparent. To me, this delayed recognition creates an unsettling tension: the same colors that invite us to look can also distract us from the person within the scene.

Wednesday / Artist of the day

Hurvin Anderson

Hurvin Anderson is a British painter of Jamaican heritage whose work explores memory, identity, and belonging through landscapes and spaces of everyday social life. His paintings often feature Caribbean scenery, barbershops, and views interrupted by fences or decorative grilles. I am drawn to how his layered colors and patterns make these spaces feel both inviting and distant, connecting with my own interest in using landscape to express identity.

Artist and artwork information found at Thomas Dane Gallery.

On color

Anderson’s layered greens make these landscapes feel lush without making them entirely peaceful. In Genera Palmarum, the contrast between light and dark foliage draws my eye into the vegetation, while its density makes the space difficult to enter visually. Looking across Constructed View, Genera Palmarum, and Shanty Town, I am interested in how repeated shapes and overlapping colors complicate the view rather than presenting a simple tropical scene. To me, this creates a feeling of familiarity mixed with distance.
